# Speaker notes — *Wie niet sterk is, moet slim zijn*

Target 40 minutes. Minute marks are cumulative targets at the *start* of each slide. Every number carries its
source: a file under `presentation/data/`, a repository note, or a figure script. Host for all fresh numbers:
ccqlin038 (2× Xeon Gold 6244, 16 cores / 32 threads, 1 MiB L2 per core), 2026-09-06, commit on branch
`presentation`. Working point: 127-qubit heavy-hex kicked Ising, θzz = −π/2, θh = 5π/16, 10 Trotter steps
(2710 rotations), observable Z₆₂, Heisenberg, coefficient threshold 2⁻¹² → peak 1.07 M terms.

| 4 | Symplectic encoding | 3:00 | 48 bytes per term at 128 qubits (32 B key + 16 B complex coefficient) is the number the second act hangs on. Product is XOR plus a phase; commutation is two ANDs and a popcount. |
| 5 | Clifford = permutation | 5:00 | Term count invariant; a Clifford layer is a table lookup per term. The XOR-delta framing is what the hash exploits later. |
| 6 | Beyond Clifford | 6:15 | The split: cos θ Q + sin θ (iPQ). Every kick layer doubles the anticommuting part. Truncation makes it approximate but controlled; noise makes it easier (`examples/b2_noisy_verification`). |
| 7 | The exponential wall | 7:45 | F0 (`data/term_growth_10steps.jsonl`, `plots/fig0_term_growth.py` with `TROTTER_STEPS=10`). Peak terms ≈ 1.4·ε^−1.5 at 5 steps. Note the backward light cone: nothing happens for four steps, then the sum jumps; at 10 steps the sum peaks and then decays as truncation eats it. ε = 2⁻¹² → 1.07 M peak, half the layers above 10⁵. |

| 18 | The idea | 20:30 | h(v) = Hv over GF(2); h(v⊕d) = h(v)⊕h(d). Contrast with a general hash (scatters everywhere) and coordinate projection (collapses under weight truncation). `ARCHITECTURE.md §Bucketing`. |
| 19 | Consequences | 22:30 | Diagram `docs/figures/design/bucket-cosets.svg`. Cosets of span(h(D)) are closed tasks; write-disjoint; dedup bucket-local; no global sort. Rayon steals cosets. |
| 20 | Load balance | 24:30 | Universal hash bound; rank(H|D) = dim D with probability ≥ 1 − 2^(dim D − b); ~10 % of two-qubit placements rank-deficient at B = 128 (`research/notes/2026-09-01-bucket-cliff.md`). Policy: 1024 terms per bucket, floor 128, grow-only. |
| 21 | Results | 26:00 | F3 = `figures/fig3_all.svg`, F1 stage 7 (`data/thread_scaling.jsonl`, `data/engine_ladder.jsonl`). Bucketed 1 thread 16.2 s (3.1× over the map); 16 threads 1.45 s (10.5×); 32 threads 1.45–1.9 s (hyperthreads add nothing). Total 26× over the naive baseline. Fact sheet: 11–13× on pure rotation layers. |
| 22 | Bucket size decides | 28:00 | F4b = `figures/fig4b_bucket_speedup.svg` (`data/bucket_sweep.jsonl`, 1 and 16 threads). 16-thread speedup by target bucket length: 256 → 7.0×, 1024 → 10.4×, 4096 → 11.2×, 16 384 → 7.7×, 65 536 → 2.5×, 262 144 → 1.7×. Parallel efficiency (busy / (loop wall × threads)) falls from 0.9 to 0.37 over the same range, so part of the collapse is task starvation (B = 32 → 16 cosets), part is the memory system. **Honesty note:** no superlinear speedup was observed on this host and circuit (appendix slide); the claim in the original storyline is not supported by the data collected here. |
| 23 | Mechanism | 30:30 | F5 = `figures/fig5_bucket_sweep.svg` (`data/bucket_sweep.jsonl`, `data/bucket_sweep_perf.jsonl`). One thread: 20 ns per term-layer, flat from 2⁸ to 2¹⁸ terms per bucket — latency-bound regardless. Single-thread L2 demand-miss rate: 29 % at 256, 6 % at 4096, 15 % at 262 144; LLC miss rate falls monotonically with coarser buckets. Sixteen threads: 2 ns per term-layer at 4096, 12 ns at 262 144. The gather run (fanout × bucket) is what must fit; dense two-qubit PTMs (fanout 16) hit the write ceiling at 16 threads (fact sheet). |
| 24 | Where the time goes | 32:30 | `docs/figures/design/phase-shares.svg`, `roofline-threads.svg` (`research/notes/2026-09-01-roofline-ccqlin038.md`). Sparse layers latency-bound at half the write ceiling; the single-thread byte model over-counts DRAM traffic 2.5–13× because the working set is cache-served — the design's point. |
| 25 | Footprint and standing | 34:00 | F7 = `figures/fig6_memory.svg` (`data/memory.jsonl`): 93 B per peak term bucketed vs 239 plain map, 277 mergesort, 828 per-thread maps; the SoA row is 48 B. `docs/figures/comparisons/baseline-ops.svg`. vs PauliPropagation.jl: 2–3× single-threaded, 95 vs 479 B per peak term (`research/notes/2026-09-01-jl-optimization-history.md`); below ~2000 terms its hash map wins, so the engine switches to one. |

Numbers the slide leaves out: cut partition rows leave **4 of 271** heavy-hex layers per step remote against 139 for a random split; in-process P=2 on this host is **−20.7 % / −14.5 %** per step at 16 / 32 threads against the single-process engine, 3/3 paired runs (`research/notes/2026-09-09-partition-row-tuning-results.md`); one partition per MPI rank gives **125 / 133 / 136 ms** per step at 2 / 4 / 8 ranks, cut rows 1.9–2.4× over random rows. Close on "wie sterk is, moet ook slim zijn". |
