# v0.2 results — the bucketed engine, measured

*2026-08-26. Slices C.1-C.3 of `research/plans/2026-08-26-v0.2-tdd-slices.md`.
Host, toolchain and caveats as in `2026-08-26-v0.1-baselines.md` (unpinned
`powersave` governor, ~5% noise floor). Raw output under
`benchmarks/results/2026-08-26-ccqlin038/v02-*`.*

Every v0.1/v0.2 pair below was measured **in the same criterion run**, on the same
inputs, so the comparison is not confounded by machine state. `apply_layer` is the
v0.1 whole-sum pipeline (still present as the oracle);
`apply_layer_bucketed` is the v0.2 engine.

## 1. Per-layer cost, n = 10⁶, 32 threads

| channel | v0.1 | v0.2 | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Depolarizing` (key-preserving) | 60.3 ms | **1.12 ms** | **54×** |
| `Clifford1Q::h` (bijection, fanout 1) | 61.4 ms | **4.81 ms** | **12.8×** |
| `Clifford2Q::cnot` (bijection, 2 qubits) | 61.0 ms | **7.06 ms** | **8.6×** |
| `PauliRotation` (fanout 2, non-injective) | 194.6 ms | **6.83 ms** | **28.5×** |

For reference, `PauliSum::add` — a bare linear merge over the same payload — is
19.0 ms. The bucketed rotation layer is now **faster than a single linear merge
of the sum**, which is the clearest statement that the global sort is gone.

`Depolarizing` gets 54× because it is key-preserving and takes the in-place
rescale path (v0.2 §6): no gather, no sort, no merge. In v0.1 it paid a full
`O(n log n)` sort to multiply each coefficient by a scalar.

Note the ordering flip: under v0.1 a rotation cost 3.2× a Clifford; under v0.2 it
costs slightly *less* than `CNOT`, because cost is now dominated by bucket fan-in
(2 for a rotation, 4 for `CNOT`) rather than by output cardinality.

## 2. Thread scaling — the headline

One `PauliRotation` layer, n = 10⁶:

| threads | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v0.1 | 254.6 | 225.8 | 207.8 | 193.9 | 189.4 | 183.1 ms |
| v0.2 | 63.4 | 32.5 | 17.6 | 10.2 | 8.30 | **8.00 ms** |
| v0.1 speedup | 1.00× | 1.13× | 1.23× | 1.31× | 1.34× | 1.39× |
| v0.2 speedup | 1.00× | 1.95× | 3.61× | 6.23× | 7.64× | **7.93×** |

**Serial fraction: ~74% → ~8%** (Amdahl at p = 32). v0.1 scope §9 claimed
"near-linear scaling up to the memory bandwidth limit" and delivered 1.39×; v0.2
delivers 7.9×, and the residual flattening past 8 threads is consistent with the
host's two NUMA nodes and memory bandwidth — i.e. it now really is the bandwidth
limit §9 talked about.

v0.2 single-threaded is already **4.0×** faster than v0.1 single-threaded, so the
win is not only parallelism: the per-bucket sort is over ~1k L2-resident terms
instead of 10⁶ DRAM-resident ones, and the per-layer allocations are gone.

## 3. End to end: the 2D Ising quench

| lattice | layers | v0.1 | v0.2 | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4×4 | 1 920 | 21.2 s | **5.5 s** | **3.85×** |
| 6×6 | 4 320 | 136.1 s | **29.0 s** | **4.69×** |

Output is byte-identical to the committed CSVs at every step below. Progression
across the rewrite, for the 6×6:

| | 6×6 | note |
|---|---|---|
| v0.1 | 136.1 s | |
| B.7 | 51.5 s | bucketed, sequential bucket loop |
| C.1 + C.3 | 31.4 s | parallel; tree merge; parallel `TopN` |
| C.4 | **29.0 s** | example stays bucketed; `rebucket` targets `desired_bits` |

The end-to-end gain is smaller than the per-layer gain (4.7× vs 8-54×), and it is
worth being precise about why rather than hand-waving: the quench runs `TopN`
after *every* one of the 4320 channels, so that cost is multiplied by the layer
count exactly as a layer's is, and it is only 4× faster (§4) rather than 8-54×.

## 4. What C.3 changed, and by how much

Two costs the C.1 measurement exposed, neither of which was on the Phase A radar:

**`into_sum` was the single worst operation in the design.** A `BinaryHeap`-based
`B`-way merge cost **147 ms** at 10⁶ terms with 1024 buckets — 147 ns per element,
because every pop does `log2(B)` comparisons against 32-byte keys scattered over
1024 runs, and a heap is inherently sequential. Replaced with `log2(B)` rounds of
pairwise merges, parallel across pairs within a round: **78.7 ms**. It does the
same `O(n log B)` comparisons but streams two sorted runs at a time and
parallelizes, trading `log B` passes over the payload for the loss of random
access. There is more available here (a radix partition on the top key bits would
be `O(n)` per pass) but the tree merge was the cheap correct fix.

**`from_sum`: 73.0 → 47.8 ms**, by running the hashing pass in parallel. Hashing
is `b × 2W` popcount-parity ops per term and is the only place in the whole design
where the hash is evaluated at all (v0.2 §2.6). The scatter stays sequential:
buckets are separate allocations, so a parallel scatter would need every thread
writing into every bucket.

**`TopN::finalize_layer_bucketed`: parallelized**, and now **4.0× faster than the
flat implementation** it replaces (22.6 ms vs 91.0 ms at 10⁶, keeping 80%). This
matters more than it looks: `propagate` runs `finalize_layer` after every channel,
so its cost is multiplied by the layer count exactly as a layer's is. The
`select_nth_unstable_by` remains sequential — it is `O(n)` with good constants and
Rayon has no parallel selection — while the magnitude gather and the per-bucket
compaction are parallel.

## 5. Where v0.2 is *slower*, and why

`propagate_trotter_step` regressed and has not been fixed:

| starting n | v0.1 | v0.2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 000 | 137.0 ms | 189.2 ms |
| 10 000 | 863.8 ms | 1.195 s |

This is a **short circuit with no truncation and a rapidly growing sum**: 64
channels, `AlwaysKeep`, so the sum grows layer over layer and the final `into_sum`
runs on the largest sum it will ever see. The bucketed engine pays a fixed
per-`propagate` conversion (`from_sum` + `into_sum` = 126 ms at 10⁶) that v0.1
does not, and 64 layers is too few to amortize it when the sum is also growing.

The crossover, roughly: conversion is worth it once
`layers × (v0.1_layer_cost − v0.2_layer_cost) > conversion_cost`. For a rotation
at 10⁶ that is `layers × 188 ms > 126 ms`, i.e. **one layer** — at *fixed* size.
The regression here comes from the size growing by orders of magnitude *within*
the call, so the conversion is paid at the maximum size while most layers ran at a
small one.

Two honest consequences:

1. A caller running many short `propagate` calls over a large sum should stay
   bucketed across calls rather than round-tripping. That is the argument for
   making `BucketedSum` public, which v0.2 §11.6 left open — this measurement
   settles it in favour.
2. The `apply_layer` / `apply_layer_adjoint` single-layer entry points still use
   the v0.1 pipeline, and for a *single* layer on a flat sum that is the right
   choice: one layer cannot amortize a conversion.

## 6. C.4 — the constants, finally measured

### `TARGET_BUCKET_LEN`: the guess was right

Terms per bucket, against one `PauliRotation` layer at 10⁶:

| terms/bucket | 62 500 | 15 625 | 3 906 | **976** | 244 | 61 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| time | 30.5 ms | 22.0 ms | 7.73 ms | **6.71 ms** | 7.13 ms | 9.77 ms |

The optimum is ~976, i.e. exactly `DEFAULT_TARGET_BUCKET_LEN = 1024`, which was
chosen from cache arithmetic alone (a `W=2` term is 48 B, so 1024 terms is ~48 KB
against 1 MiB of L2 per core). The curve is flat within 15% over 244-3906 and
degrades sharply outside it — 62 500 per bucket is 4.5× worse, which is the
`O(m log m)` per-bucket sort reasserting itself, and 61 per bucket is 1.5× worse
from per-bucket fixed cost and Rayon task overhead.

### The rehash hysteresis was actively harmful

`rebucket` originally used a 4×/¼× hysteresis band, on the theory that `n` swings
every layer and acting on every deviation would thrash. **Measured, that band cost
~10%**: it lets the steady state sit up to 4× above target, and on the Ising
quench it parked at 128 buckets (1562 terms each) where `desired_bits` would have
chosen 256 (781 each). Per the sweep above, that is the wrong side of the optimum.

Removed. `rebucket` now converges on `desired_bits` directly. The guard was
largely redundant anyway: bucket counts move in powers of two, so `desired_bits`
only changes when `n` crosses a doubling, which for a `TopN`-capped workload never
happens. 6×6 Ising: 34.6 s → 29.0 s.

The residual risk — a sum oscillating across a power-of-two boundary on alternate
layers, refining and coarsening at `O(n)` each — is accepted and unmeasured. No
workload in the repo exhibits it and guarding it properly needs state
`BucketedSum` does not carry. Recorded rather than assumed away.

### Staying bucketed across calls

The Ising example now holds one `BucketedSum` for the whole quench and calls
`propagate_bucketed` per Trotter step, instead of `propagate`'s convert-in/out per
step. That is the pattern §5's regression argues for, and the example is the place
to demonstrate it. It also needed `BucketedSum::expectation_product_state`, a
per-bucket parallel reduction, so the observable can be read without converting
back.

Note that reduction sums partials in bucket order rather than globally-sorted
order, so it is not bitwise equal to the flat version — float addition is not
associative. The difference is ~1e-13 against 10-decimal output, and the committed
CSVs came out byte-identical, but the docs say so explicitly rather than implying
bitwise equality.

`MIN_TERMS_PER_TASK = 64` and `MIN_BUCKETS_FOR_PARALLEL = 2` remain unswept.

## 7. §7 items still not done

Explicitly, so they are not mistaken for having been tried:

- **§7.1 `Phase`/`f64`-valued amplitude tables.** Not attempted. The prediction
  that a `Complex64` multiply would make Cliffords *slower* than v0.1's
  phase-swap was wrong at the layer level (Cliffords are 8.6-12.8× faster), so
  the motivation is much weaker than it looked.
- **§7.2 k-way run merge instead of a per-bucket sort.** Not attempted.
- **§7.3 L1 hashmap dedup per bucket.** Not attempted.
- **§7.5 scatter, or re-grouping deltas for input-bucket locality.** Not
  attempted. §6.1's read amplification is still `|h(D)|`-fold; it shows up as
  `CNOT` (4 buckets) costing more than a rotation (2 buckets) despite producing
  fewer outputs.
- **A radix-partition `into_sum`**, per §4 above.
- **NUMA first-touch placement.** Not attempted; the flattening past 8 threads is
  the symptom that would motivate it.

`TARGET_BUCKET_LEN` was swept and confirmed, and the rehash hysteresis was
measured and removed — see §6. `MIN_TERMS_PER_TASK = 64` and
`MIN_BUCKETS_FOR_PARALLEL = 2` remain unswept guesses.
